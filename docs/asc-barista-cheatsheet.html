Part 1 · The Bean — origin → harvest → processing

Origin & History

Origin
Ethiopia — arabica grows wild there. Legend: goat herder Kaldi (~9th century) notices his goats dancing.
First cultivation
Yemen, 15th century — traded through the port of Mocha (hence the name).
To Europe
17th century via Venice; coffee houses London 1652, Vienna 1683.
Today
Brazil is the largest producer (~⅓ of the world crop), then Vietnam (robusta), Colombia, Indonesia, Ethiopia.
Bean Belt
Growing belt between the tropics (~23.5° N–S): stable 18–22 °C, altitude, wet + dry season.
Altitude
Higher = cooler = slower ripening = denser bean, more acidity & complexity. SHG/SHB = Strictly High Grown, above ~1,350 m.

Top 10 Coffee Producers

  1. Brazil — ~⅓ of the world crop; arabica + robusta (conilon), naturals, espresso base
  2. Vietnam — world's #1 robusta producer; instant & blends
  3. Colombia — washed arabica, balanced; two harvests a year
  4. Indonesia — Sumatra wet-hulled: earthy, heavy body; plus robusta
  5. Ethiopia — birthplace of arabica; landraces, floral & berry
  6. Uganda — Africa's robusta powerhouse
  7. India — fine robusta, Monsooned Malabar
  8. Honduras — Central America's largest producer
  9. Peru — arabica, strong in certified organic
  10. Mexico — highland arabica ("altura")

Varieties (arabica cultivars)

Typica & Bourbon — original forms: clean, sweet, elegant · Caturra / Catuaí — high-yield mutations · SL28 / SL34 — Kenya: blackcurrant · Geisha — jasmine, bergamot, record prices · "Ethiopian Heirloom" — umbrella term for Ethiopian landraces, not a variety name.

The coffee cherry

Skin → pulp with mucilage (sugary slime layer — the key to processing) → parchment → silverskin → 2 seeds (peaberry: just one, round).

Harvest

Selective picking — only ripe cherries, several passes (specialty standard) · strip picking — whole branch at once · mechanical — Brazil, flat terrain. Unripe beans ("quakers") taste like straw and paper and cannot be brewed away.

Processing

How much fruit stays on the bean while it dries — the biggest flavour lever before roasting.

Washed · wet De-pulp → ferment 12–72 h → wash → dry. Clean, clear, acidity-driven, shows the terroir. (Colombia, Kenya, Ethiopia)
Honey · pulped natural De-pulped, but the mucilage stays on during drying. Sweeter and rounder than washed, cleaner than natural. White → yellow → red → black honey = progressively more mucilage & sweetness. (Costa Rica, Brazil)
Natural · dry Whole cherry dried in the sun for 15–30 days, turned regularly, then hulled. Intensely fruity (berry), heavy, sweet, winey — risk: fermenty, vinegary off-notes. (Ethiopia, Brazil)

Rule of thumb: the more fruit stays on, the fruitier and heavier the cup — and the higher the risk of defects.

Part 2 · The Bar — grind → brew → taste → serve → clean

The Espresso Recipe · double shot (doppio), 58 mm basket — the starting point you dial in from

Dose18 ggrounds in the basket · tolerance ±1 g
Yield36 gin the cup, weighed · tolerance ±3 g
Ratio1:21 g coffee → 2 g beverage
EBF50 %dose ÷ yield × 100
Temp92–94 °Cbrew water
Pressure9 barmore gains nothing
Time from pump start · tolerance ±3 s Light 27–30 s Medium 24–26 s Dark 20–23 s

EBF (Espresso Brewing Formula) says the same thing as the ratio, just as a percentage: 1:2 = 50 %, 1:2.5 = 40 %. Why dark runs shorter: dark roasts are porous and dissolve fast — they tip into bitterness early, so shorter and cooler. Light roasts are dense — they need longer, hotter, possibly a wider ratio.
Shot family: Ristretto 1:1–1.5 (dense, sweet — grind coarser, don't just stop early) · Espresso 1:2–2.5 · Lungo 1:3–4 (lighter, more bitterness).

Factors

What you adjust — and what it does.

Grind Size
The strongest lever. Finer = slower & more extraction, coarser = faster & less. This is how you correct the shot time. Espresso: fine sand (200–400 µm).
Dose
Ground coffee in the basket. 58 mm basket: 16–20 g. Too much → no headspace (air gap below the shower screen) → channeling. Too little → the bed floats and tilts.
Yield
Beverage mass in grams on the scale — never in ml, crema fakes the volume.
Time
A result, not a setting. If it's off → change the grind. Measured from pump start.
Distribution
WDT: stir the grounds with fine needles 8–12 times, then level. The most effective step against channeling (water punching through one path instead of the whole bed).
Tamping
Level beats hard. Beyond ~10–15 kg more force changes nothing — but 1° of tilt creates a channel. Never tap afterwards.
Temperature
Fine-tuning tool: light 94–96 · medium 92–94 · dark 88–92 °C. ±1 °C ≈ 0.2–0.3 points of extraction yield.
Pressure
9 bar is the standard. Above 9 bar only the channeling risk grows.

Espresso Workflow

  1. Wipe the portafilter dry — leftover moisture starts local extraction
  2. Grind 18.0 g, check on the scale (±0.1 g)
  3. WDT: 8–12 circular strokes, break up clumps
  4. Level — flat, even surface
  5. Tamp: level > hard — do not tap afterwards
  6. Flush the group head for 2 s (stabilises temperature, clears residue)
  7. Lock in and start immediately — the puck heats up otherwise
  8. Cup on the scale, tare, timer runs from pump start
  9. Stop at target yield (allow ~1–2 g of drip-through)
  10. Knock out the puck, rinse the basket, wipe the bench

Extraction: under · balanced · over

Only ~30 % of the bean is soluble. Target: 18–22 % of the bean in the cup (EY = extraction yield). Acids & fruit dissolve early, sweetness & body in the middle, bitter compounds late.

Under-extracted — too little dissolved sharp-sour, salty (the most reliable sign), thin, finish cuts off abruptly. → Grind finer; then: hotter, wider ratio.
Balanced — the target Sweetness carries, acidity lively but integrated, round body, long clean finish. EY 18–22 %, espresso TDS 8–11 %.
Over-extracted — too much dissolved bitter, astringent (dry, furry mouthfeel like black tea), ashy, finish lingers unpleasantly. → Grind coarser; then: cooler, tighter ratio.
Sour AND bitter at the same time? Uneven extraction (channeling) — fix technique (WDT, level tamp), not the grind.

Sensory: the 5 Dimensions

Acidity
Liveliness, freshness, "juiciness" — sides of the tongue. Judged by quality, not quantity. Acidity + sweetness = origin character; acidity + salt = under-extraction.
Sweetness
Caramel/fruit sweetness developed in the roast — tip of the tongue. The most reliable sign of a good extraction.
Body
Texture and weight in the mouth, thin ↔ syrupy — whole mouth. Body ≠ strength.
Flavor
The overall aromatic impression (fruit, chocolate, florals …) — mostly retronasal, i.e. "smelled" through the back of the throat as you exhale.
Finishaftertaste
What stays, how long, how pleasant. Good: sweet, clean, long. Short & abrupt = under-extracted; dry-bitter lingering = over-extracted.
Stir before tasting — an espresso is layered. Acidity is not a defect: a light-roast Kenyan is supposed to taste bright.

Milk & Latte Art

TemperatureMeaning
55–65 °CTarget window (SCA exam standard); sweetness optimum 55–62 °C
~60 °Chand test: the pitcher becomes uncomfortably hot to hold
> 70 °Cscorched: proteins denature, foam collapses, tastes "cooked" — can't be saved, never re-steam

Two phases

1 · Stretching: tip just below the surface, steady paper-like "tss" — introduce air only until the pitcher is body-warm (~37 °C). All the volume is created here.
2 · Rolling: tip deeper and off-centre → a stable whirlpool pulls the bubbles under, up to target temperature. No more air.

Microfoam check: glossy like wet paint, no visible bubbles. Tap the pitcher, swirl, pour promptly — foam separates after ~30 s.

Latte art: the prerequisite is texture, not pouring technique. Tilt the cup, start high (milk dives under the crema), from about half full move in close and let the foam float up, finish with a pull-through.

Whole milk = finer, more stable foam (latte art) · skim = more but coarser foam · oat: barista version, max ~60 °C. Hygiene: wipe the wand with a damp cloth + purge briefly after every use.

Drinks — base: double shot, 36 g

Sorted by size — cup height = drink size. All cups share the same width, and the espresso layer is identical in every drink: one double shot, 36 g. Only water, milk and foam change.

espresso steamed milk foam crema espresso + water

Flat white and cappuccino are nearly the same size — the real difference is the amount of air (overrun = volume gained during steaming). Steam too airy and your flat white automatically becomes a cappuccino.

Cappuccino, classic definition: ⅓ espresso · ⅓ milk · ⅓ foam (the original ~150 ml serve). At café sizes the shot stays a doppio — the thick foam cap is what defines it.

Americano: hot water onto the shot · Long Black: water first, shot on top → crema survives.

Cleaning — Step by Step

Rancid coffee oils make every shot dull and bitter — cleaning is taste maintenance, and hygiene is part of the assessment.

After every shot

  1. Knock the puck into the knock box
  2. Rinse basket & portafilter under the group, wipe dry
  3. Wipe the steam wand with its damp cloth + purge briefly

Daily (close of day)

  1. Backflush with the blind basket, water only, ~5 cycles
  2. Brush the group head & gasket, wipe the shower screen
  3. Steam wand: wipe, purge — soak the tip if milk is baked on
  4. Empty and rinse drip tray and knock box
  5. Wipe bench and machine body; separate cloths for wand / bench / portafilter

Weekly

  1. Backflush with espresso detergent, then several water-only rinses
  2. Soak baskets & portafilter heads in detergent solution (not the handle)
  3. Grinder: empty hopper, brush out chute and burrs — old grounds go rancid
  4. Monthly: unscrew the shower screen and deep-clean it

Rules of Thumb

  • One variable per change — otherwise you learn nothing.
  • Time is a result. The grind is the dial.
  • Level beats hard — when tamping, flatness is what counts.
  • Weigh everything. Volume lies, mass doesn't.
  • Sour + salty = too little · bitter + dry = too much · sour + bitter = technique.
  • Acidity is not a defect — acidity without sweetness is.
