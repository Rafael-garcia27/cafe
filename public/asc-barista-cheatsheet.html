Part 1 · The Bean — origin → harvest → processing

Origin & History

17th c. via Venice · London 1652 · Vienna 1683.

Brazil #1 (~⅓ of world crop) · Vietnam · Colombia · Indonesia · Ethiopia.
Bean Belt
Between the tropics, ~23.5° N–S. Stable 18–22 °C, wet + dry season.
Altitude
Higher = slower ripening = denser bean = more acidity & complexity. SHG = above ~1,350 m.

  8. Honduras — Central America's largest
  9. Peru — organic-certified arabica
  10. Mexico — highland arabica ("altura")

The coffee cherry

Skin→ Pulp + mucilage→ Parchment→ Silverskin→ 2 seeds

Mucilage = sugary layer, the key to processing · peaberry = one round seed.

Harvest

Selective picking — only ripe cherries, several passes = specialty standard · strip — whole branch · mechanical — Brazil. Unripe "quakers" taste papery — can't be brewed away.

Processing

How much fruit stays on while drying — the biggest flavour lever before roasting.

Washed · wet De-pulp → ferment 12–72 h → wash → dry.
Clean, bright, terroir-true. Colombia, Kenya, Ethiopia
Honey · pulped natural De-pulped, mucilage stays on to dry.
Sweeter than washed, cleaner than natural. White → yellow → red → black = more mucilage. Costa Rica, Brazil
Natural · dry Whole cherry sun-dried 15–30 d, then hulled.
Berry-fruity, heavy, winey. Risk: ferment, vinegar. Ethiopia, Brazil

More fruit left on = fruitier + heavier cup — and riskier.

Time from pump start · ±3 s Light 27–30 s Medium 24–26 s Dark 20–23 s
EBF
The ratio as a percentage: 1:2 = 50 % · 1:2.5 = 40 %.
Why dark = shorter
Porous → extracts fast → tips into bitterness early. Light = dense → longer, hotter, wider.
Shot family
Ristretto 1:1–1.5 (coarser grind, not stopped early) · Espresso 1:2–2.5 · Lungo 1:3–4.

Factors

What you adjust — and what it does.

Grind Size
Strongest lever. Finer = slower + more extraction. Fixes the shot time. Fine sand, 200–400 µm.
Dose
58 mm basket: 16–20 g. Too much → no headspace → channeling. Too little → bed tilts.
Yield
Weigh in grams, never ml — crema fakes volume.
Time
A result, not a setting. Off? → change the grind.
Distribution
WDT: stir 8–12× with needles, then level. Best weapon against channeling.
Tamping
Level beats hard. ~10–15 kg is plenty · 1° tilt = channel · never tap after.
Temperature
Light 94–96 · medium 92–94 · dark 88–92 °C. ±1 °C ≈ ±0.2–0.3 EY.
Pressure
9 bar. More only adds channeling.

Espresso Workflow

  1. Wipe portafilter dry
  2. Grind 18.0 g — reweigh, ±0.1 g
  3. WDT: 8–12 strokes
  4. Level the surface
  5. Tamp level — never tap after
  6. Flush group 2 s
  7. Lock in, start immediately
  8. Cup on scale, tare — time runs from pump start
  9. Stop at target yield (−1–2 g for drip-through)
  10. Knock out, rinse, wipe

Extraction: under · balanced · over

~30 % of the bean is soluble — target 18–22 % in the cup (EY = extraction yield). Early: acids & fruit · middle: sweetness & body · late: bitterness.

Under — too little dissolved Sharp-sour · salty (surest sign) · thin · finish cuts off.
→ Grind finer. Then: hotter, wider ratio.
Balanced — the target Sweetness carries · acidity integrated · round body · long clean finish.
EY 18–22 % · espresso TDS 8–11 %.
Over — too much dissolved Bitter · astringent (dry, furry) · ashy · finish lingers.
→ Grind coarser. Then: cooler, tighter ratio.
Sour AND bitter? Channeling — fix technique (WDT, level tamp), not the grind.

Sensory: the 5 Dimensions

Acidity
Sides of the tongue. Liveliness, "juiciness". Judge quality, not quantity. + sweetness = character · + salt = under-extracted.
Sweetness
Tip of the tongue. Caramel/fruit from the roast — surest sign of good extraction.
Body
Whole mouth. Texture & weight: thin ↔ syrupy. Body ≠ strength.
Flavor
The aromatic impression — fruit, chocolate, florals. Smelled retronasally (back of the throat).
Finish
What stays & how long. Good: sweet, clean, long. Abrupt = under · dry-bitter = over.
Stir before tasting — the shot is layered. Acidity ≠ defect: a Kenyan should taste bright.

Milk & Latte Art

TempMeaning
55–65 °CTarget window (SCA standard) · sweetness peaks 55–62 °C
~60 °CHand test: pitcher gets uncomfortably hot
> 70 °CScorched — foam collapses, tastes "cooked". Start over, never re-steam

Steaming — two phases

  1. Stretch: tip just under the surface, paper-like "tss" — air only until pitcher is body-warm (~37 °C)
  2. Roll: tip deeper, off-centre — whirlpool, no more air, up to ~60 °C

Microfoam check

Glossy like wet paint, no visible bubbles. Tap, swirl, pour within ~30 s.

Latte art

  1. Microfoam first — texture beats technique
  2. Tilt cup, start high — milk dives under the crema
  3. Half full: go low & close — foam floats up
  4. Finish with a pull-through

Whole milk = finest, most stable foam · skim = more but coarse · oat: barista version, ≤ 60 °C.

After every use: wipe the wand + purge.

Drinks — base: double shot, 36 g

Sorted by size. Same cup width everywhere — the espresso layer is identical in every drink; only water, milk and foam change.

espresso steamed milk foam crema espresso + water

Flat white vs. cappuccino = same size, different air (overrun). Too much foam turns one into the other.

Classic cappuccino rule: ⅓ espresso · ⅓ milk · ⅓ foam (original ~150 ml serve).

Long Black: water first, shot on top → crema survives.

Cleaning — Step by Step

Rancid coffee oils = dull, bitter shots. Hygiene is graded.

After every shot

  1. Knock out the puck
  2. Rinse basket & portafilter, wipe dry
  3. Wipe wand + purge

Daily — close of day

  1. Backflush blind basket, water only, ~5×
  2. Brush group head & gasket
  3. Wand: wipe, purge — soak tip if crusted
  4. Empty drip tray & knock box
  5. Wipe bench — separate cloths: wand / bench / portafilter

Weekly

  1. Backflush with detergent, rinse well
  2. Soak baskets & portafilter head (not the handle)
  3. Grinder: empty & brush hopper, chute, burrs
  4. Monthly: shower screen off, deep-clean

Rules of Thumb

  • One variable per change — otherwise you learn nothing.
  • Time is a result. The grind is the dial.
  • Level beats hard — when tamping, flatness is what counts.
  • Weigh everything. Volume lies, mass doesn't.
  • Sour + salty = too little · bitter + dry = too much · sour + bitter = technique.
  • Acidity is not a defect — acidity without sweetness is.
